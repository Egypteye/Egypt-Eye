# Editing the Proposals page

**flyingdressesegypt.com/studio → ✿ Flying Dresses — Proposals page**

Already filled in with exactly what the page says today. Nothing changed when
this went in.

## What you can change

- **Headline** — the small line, the headline and the paragraph under it.
- **What is included** — the seven entries. Reorder by dragging.
- **What you can personalise** — the four entries.
- **The sequence** — the seven numbered steps. The order in the list is the
  order on the page.
- **The venue** — how the rooftop is described, and the "not inside the
  archaeological complex" paragraph.
- **Decoration designs** — empty today. See below.
- **The photographs** — how many, what the set covers, how they arrive.

Emptying a list removes that section. That is the only way to drop something
you no longer offer, so it is deliberate rather than a bug. Clearing a single
text box does the opposite: it goes back to the wording in the code.

## The decoration catalogue is the big one

It is empty, and the page handles that honestly right now: it explains how
choosing works and offers to send what is currently available.

Add a design here — id, name, description and **a photograph** — and it
appears on the page as a real choice that carries into the enquiry, so your
team knows which setup somebody picked. This is the field that turns "we will
send you what is available" into an actual catalogue.

The photograph is required. An entry without one is a list item pretending to
be a design, and a customer choosing between names cannot choose. If an entry
is missing its photograph or its id, the site drops that entry rather than
rendering it broken.

## The three switches

The venue's name, the street address and the changing room are **recorded but
not on the page**. Each has a switch, and each switch is off.

Turn one on only when you have actually checked it:

| Switch | Check before turning it on |
|---|---|
| **Publish the venue's name** | The hotel is confirmed, the rooftop arrangement is current, and they are happy to be named publicly. Naming them is a claim about somebody else's business as well as yours. |
| **Publish the street address** | The address is exact. This is what somebody types into a map on the morning of their proposal. If any part of it is approximate, a couple arrives at the wrong gate on the day. |
| **Publish the changing room** | It is guaranteed, not "may be available". Published as a flat promise, it becomes something a guest already in a dress discovers is untrue at the venue. |

With a switch off, the page prints wording that is true either way. You cannot
half-publish one: a switch turned on with an empty box beside it publishes
nothing rather than a blank space where a hotel's name should be.

## Not here, on purpose

**The deposit.** It is 25%, and it stays in the code with the other money
authorities. The percentage and the sentence spelling it out in words would be
two places holding one number, and they would eventually disagree. Ask and I
will change it.

**A price.** There isn't one. No figure has been set for a proposal and the
page says so. A box here would just be an invitation to invent one.
