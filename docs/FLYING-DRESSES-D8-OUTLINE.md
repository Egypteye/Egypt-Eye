# D8 — Colour and skin tone: editorial outline

20 November 2026. Outline only, for your approval before drafting.

You kept this one in and set the condition: no rigid beauty rules, nothing
suggesting a colour is unsuitable for a particular skin tone, inclusive and
genuinely helpful.

That condition is not a softening of the article. **It is the article.** The
standard version of this piece is built on a false premise, and saying so
plainly is both the honest position and the more useful one.

---

## The premise I want to write from

> Every colour in this collection works on everybody. What changes is what the
> photographer does about the light, and that is our job rather than yours.

This is not diplomacy. It is what actually happens on the roof. A flying dress
photograph is a person at distance in front of a very bright background, with
several square metres of coloured fabric bouncing light up into their face.
The variables that decide whether the picture works are exposure, the angle of
the sun, and what the fabric is reflecting. None of those is a property of the
person.

The conventional "winter/summer palette" advice is retail advice, written for
someone standing under shop lighting choosing an everyday garment. It
transfers badly to a photograph taken an hour before sunset in the desert, and
pretending otherwise would be giving confident guidance that is wrong.

---

## Proposed structure

### 1. The question people are actually asking
Open by naming it honestly: "will I look good in this" rather than "what is my
palette". The rest of the article answers the real question.

### 2. Why the usual advice does not transfer
Shop lighting against late sun at Giza. Fixed garment against five metres of
moving fabric. Looking at yourself in a mirror against being photographed at
twenty metres with the plateau behind you. Three different problems.

### 3. What the fabric does to the light on your face
The genuinely useful, first-party part. Satin at this scale is a reflector. A
saturated dress throws its own colour upward, which warms or cools the light
on the face depending on the colour, and more so when the train is spread on
the ground around you in bright sun. This is real, it is visible in our own
frames, and almost nobody writing about flying dresses mentions it.

### 4. The two things that actually change the photograph
- **Exposure.** A very dark dress and a very bright background pull in
  opposite directions, and the photographer is choosing which to protect.
  That is a decision made on the roof, and it is why the dark colours are
  shot differently rather than less well.
- **The hour.** Hard overhead sun is the one condition that is unkind to
  everybody and every colour. Nothing in a palette chart fixes that; moving
  the session by two hours does.

### 5. What to choose on instead
A short, positive list. Choose on the photograph you want: loud and graphic,
or tonal and soft. Choose on the location. Choose on the occasion. Choose on
the colour you actually like wearing, which is the one you will stand in
confidently, and confidence is visible in a frame in a way that undertone is
not.

### 6. What we will do on the day
Close on the operator's promise: tell the photographer what you are worried
about and the adjustment is theirs to make. Where you stand, what is behind
you, which direction the light comes from. Ten seconds of their decision,
rather than a constraint on your choice.

---

## Rules I am writing under

1. **No colour is named as unsuitable for anyone.** Not once, not hedged.
2. **No skin tone is categorised.** No undertone taxonomy, no seasons, no
   "deeper complexions suit" constructions. The article does not sort people.
3. **Every piece of advice is about light, hour or technique**, which are
   things we control, rather than about the reader's body.
4. **Illustrated across the real range in our archive**, which already spans a
   wide variety of clients, shown without comment. The pictures make the point
   that the text should not have to labour.
5. **No before/after, no "flattering", no "slimming".**

---

## The commercial case for writing it

It targets `flying dress colour skin tone`, which people genuinely search, and
every competing page answers it with retail palette advice. Being the one
result that says the premise is wrong, and then giving a better answer, is a
strong position and an unusually honest one for the category.

It also removes a real purchase blocker. Someone who thinks the colour they
want will not suit them either picks a colour they like less or does not book.
