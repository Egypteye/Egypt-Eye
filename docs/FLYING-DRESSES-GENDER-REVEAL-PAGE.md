# The Gender Reveal page: what was built, and what it needs from you

Built 25 October 2026. URL unchanged: `/flying-dresses/celebrations/gender-reveals`.

---

## 1. The finding that shaped everything

**We have exactly one photograph of a gender reveal we have run.** The Kayan
shoot: a balloon arch, lit letters, flowers, a teddy bear, the family with
their arms thrown wide, the pyramids behind them at sunset.

One. It is also one of the best photographs the business owns.

That is the opposite of the proposal page's problem, where we had none at all,
and it needed the opposite discipline. The risk here is not passing somebody
else's occasion off as a reveal. **The risk is padding.** Four supporting
frames from couples shoots, shown without comment, would read as a reveal
portfolio five times the size of the real one.

So the single real frame is given the hero, alone, full width, where it does
the most work. It is then **deliberately left out of the gallery**, and the
gallery says so in its own first sentence: that photograph is the only reveal
we have, and these four show the roof it happened on. Each of them is
captioned with the occasion it was actually photographed at.

Repeating it as the gallery lead as well would have made one photograph look
like two. It would also have made the gallery's own honesty line false.

## 2. What this page does NOT say, and why that was deliberate

You supplied a lot of operational detail for the **marriage proposal**: a 25%
deposit with the balance on the day, cash or PayPal or a payment link, more
than a hundred edited photographs, Google Drive delivery with a year of
access, a speaker on the roof, a setup built before the couple arrives, a
hotel name and a street.

**None of that is on this page, and none of it is in its content file.**

It was not an oversight and it is not laziness. Those facts were supplied
about the proposal. Nothing of the kind has ever been said about a gender
reveal, which is a different service: a family event, with guests, a
supplier-dependent cake, and a venue that has to approve whatever happens in
the air above its roof. Published terms borrowed from a private sunrise
proposal would be wrong in the way a customer discovers at the point of paying.

**The build fails if any of it reappears here.** There are checks for a price,
a percentage, a hotel name, a street, Google Drive, a year of access, a
photograph count and a speaker, plus a structural one: this page is forbidden
from importing the proposal's content file at all, so the next person cannot
reintroduce one by accident.

If any of those terms *do* apply to a gender reveal, that is a fine thing to
tell me. It just has to be said rather than assumed.

## 4. Reveal methods: why there is no menu

Every competitor publishes one: balloon pop, confetti cannon, smoke, a box of
butterflies. **We cannot, and you are the one who said so.** Your own record
says it twice:

> "The reveal mechanism is agreed case by case with the venue. We will not
> confirm a particular prop or effect until they have."

> "What reveal mechanisms the venues actually permit. Nothing specific should
> be advertised until this is answered."

So the section explains **how a method gets agreed** instead of listing methods
as available: the venue decides what is allowed in the air, it can differ by
date and by wind, we ask them before agreeing it with you, and your own cake or
props are usually fine but depend on the venue.

That is not a weaker section. It answers the question underneath the menu,
which is "will the thing I saw on Instagram actually be allowed on a hotel roof
in Giza", and a visitor who reads it knows more than one who read a list.

**A check fails the build if the page names confetti, smoke, a cannon,
fireworks, sparklers or coloured powder.** The moment you tell me what the
venues permit, that list becomes a real section.

## 5. The colour theme, and the empty catalogue

**The colour theme is live and working**, because you confirmed it: "a choice
from our existing decoration designs, in a colour theme you pick."

The three options are not "pink" and "blue", and that was a deliberate call.
The person using the chooser usually does not know the answer yet, so a
pink-or-blue toggle is incoherent for the one visitor it is aimed at. The real
question is whether the decoration is allowed to give anything away:

1. Neutral, so nothing gives it away.
2. Left to whoever is holding the secret.
3. A palette you describe to us.

Choosing one carries it into the enquiry, into the email you receive and into
the WhatsApp draft, so nobody describes it twice. **Verified end to end in a
real browser**, including that a tampered-with link cannot put a theme we do
not offer into an email you would read as fact.

**The decoration catalogue is fully built and currently empty**, which is the
honest state: your own open questions include "Which decoration designs exist
for this, and photographs of them." With no entries it renders how choosing
works and offers to send the current setups. Adding a design is one edit, and
the selection and handoff already work.

## 6. Money: a quotation, not payment terms

**No price, and no payment terms.** Your record is explicit: "PRICE. Nothing
has been set for a gender reveal, with or without video."

So the section says how the figure reaches you (in writing, after we have
confirmed the roof), that payment terms come with the quotation, and that no
page on this website takes a payment. It does not invent a deposit.

The content file carries `termsSet: false` with a note. **A check fails if
anybody flips it to true**, because flipping it is exactly how a section gets
made to look finished with terms nobody agreed.

## 7. Video: the one thing this page says that the proposal page cannot

Your record lists a cinematic film of the reveal, slow motion and vertical
reels as **in-house**. The proposal brief told me not to promise video unless
it was confirmed for that service, so the proposal page is silent about it.
Here it is confirmed, so it has its own panel. Still no price.

## 8. What I need from you

In rough priority order:

1. **A price, or a from-price**, with and without video. This is the single
   biggest conversion lever on the page and every competitor publishes one.
2. **What the venues actually permit for the reveal itself.** This turns
   section 4 from an explanation into a section that sells.
3. **The decoration designs for gender reveals**, one photograph per design
   plus the name you call it. Turns an empty section into a browsable
   catalogue and a working selection.
4. **Payment terms for this service.** Whether the proposal's 25/75 applies,
   or something else does.
5. **More gender reveal photographs.** The setup before anyone arrives, the
   moment itself, the reaction, the family afterwards. We have one frame
   carrying the whole page.
6. **Whether cake and props are reliably available** through a supplier, and
   which supplier.
7. **Which roof this happens on.** The proposal's venue name is recorded and
   held pending your verification; nothing has been said about where a gender
   reveal is held, so this page names no venue at all.
8. **Photograph delivery for this service**: roughly how many, how they
   arrive, how long they stay available. The page currently says only that
   they are colour-graded and delivered after the session, which is all your
   record says.
