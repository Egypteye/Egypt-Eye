## What a proposal on the roof actually looks like, start to finish

Most of what we do for a proposal happens before either of you is on the roof.

That is the thing worth understanding first, because it is the difference
between a proposal that is photographed and a proposal that is staged again
afterwards for the camera. By the time you walk out, the setup is standing,
the candles are lit, the music is cued and the photographer is already in
position. Nothing is being assembled while she watches.

### The roof

A private hotel rooftop in Giza looking across at the Great Pyramids. It is
confirmed with the venue for your date and your hour before anything is
agreed, which is the step that has to happen first: the roof is somebody
else's building and their answer is the one that decides your evening.

The pyramids are the view from it. They are not a backdrop we have placed you
in front of, and the difference shows in a wide frame, where the plateau sits
at its real distance with the roofs of the town between.

We do not name the hotel on this site yet. The name was given to us and we
are waiting to confirm that they are content to be named publicly before we
publish it, which is a slower answer than most people want and the right one.

### What three of them actually looked like

We have photographed proposals on this roof with three different setups, and
putting them side by side is more useful than any list.

The first was built around a **heart of red and white roses** on a frame, tall
enough to stand inside. A red patterned rug on the deck, rose petals scattered
across it, and a ring of white pillar candles in glass holders around the
whole thing. Shot in soft haze, so the three pyramids behind sat pale and the
roses were the only strong colour in the frame.

The second was quieter: **two uprights carrying white drapes**, small clusters
of flowers at the top corners, the fabric running down onto the deck. Clear
blue sky that evening. It is the one that photographs most like a wedding, and
it is the one where the plateau does most of the work, because there is less
in front of it.

The third was the most elaborate: a **heart of roses with a gold script sign
inside it**, white drape to one side, and a red carpet runner down the deck.
Both of them in white. It is the loudest of the three and it is the one that
reads instantly in a photograph on a phone.

Read those as what was built on three particular dates rather than as a menu.
You choose the setup from the designs we are running when you enquire, and we
send you what is actually available for your date, because what is in the
collection changes.

### The secrecy, which is the actual job

A proposal is planned the same way a surprise party is, except that one
misdirected message ruins it rather than spoils it.

You pick the channel. Email, WhatsApp, whichever you name, and we use only
that one. Nothing goes anywhere you have not told us to send it. The venue,
the timing and the setup are arranged between us and the roof, so there is no
reason for anything to reach your partner at all.

If the address you are writing from is one you share, say so in the first
message. It is the single most common way this goes wrong and it is the
easiest to avoid.

### Your song, through the speaker

There is a speaker on the roof. Send us the track you want playing as she
arrives and we cue it.

It is a small thing that people are consistently glad they asked about, and it
costs nothing to arrange. It also solves the problem of the walk out, which is
otherwise the most awkward thirty seconds of the evening for the person doing
the asking.

### Where the photographer is

Not where she will be looking.

The frames people keep are almost always the ones taken before anybody said
anything: the approach, the moment she sees the setup, the second before the
question. Those only exist if somebody is already in position with the right
lens on, which is why the photographer is placed before you walk out rather
than following you onto the roof.

After the question, the session opens up. In all three of the sets above there
are standing portraits, a dip, a kiss, and frames of the two of them with the
plateau behind, all taken in the half hour that follows.

### What you get, and when

**More than a hundred edited photographs.** They are delivered as a Google
Drive gallery, and the gallery stays available for one year from the day it is
shared. There is no rush to download and no deadline to miss.

### How it is paid for

**Twenty-five per cent secures your date.** Until it is paid the roof is not
held, and we will say that plainly rather than let you assume otherwise.

**The remaining seventy-five per cent is paid on the day, after the event.**
Cash, PayPal, or a payment link we send you.

The quotation comes back to you in writing once the venue, the date and the
time are agreed. Nothing is held before that.

### Somewhere to change

Changing at the venue is arranged as part of holding your date. Tell us
whether you are arriving dressed or want to change there and it is confirmed
with the venue in writing along with everything else.

### The order it happens in

1. Tell us the date, the hour and what she loves.
2. Choose the setup from what we are running for your date.
3. We confirm the roof and send the quotation in writing.
4. A deposit secures it.
5. The setup goes up on the roof ahead of you. The photographer takes position.
6. You walk out. It is photographed as it happens.
7. The gallery arrives, yours for a year.

## FAQs

**Does she see anything being set up?**
No. The setup is finished on the roof before you arrive, and the photographer
is in position before you walk out. That is the whole design of the evening.

**Can I choose the decoration?**
You choose from the designs we are running for your date. We send you what is
genuinely available when you enquire rather than publishing a catalogue here
that may not match what exists on your evening.

**How do you keep it secret?**
You name one channel and we use only that one. Tell us if the address you are
writing from is shared, because that is the most common way a surprise is
lost.

**Can I have a particular song playing?**
Yes. There is a speaker on the roof. Send us the track and we cue it for the
moment she arrives.

**How many photographs do we get?**
More than a hundred, edited, delivered as a Google Drive gallery that stays
available for a year from the day it is shared.

**What do I pay, and when?**
Twenty-five per cent secures the date and the remaining seventy-five per cent
is paid on the day, after the event. Cash, PayPal, or a payment link we send
you.

**Which hotel is it?**
We are not publishing the name until the venue has confirmed they are content
to be named. The roof itself is confirmed with them for your date before
anything is agreed.
