## A gender reveal on a rooftop above Giza

The moment itself lasts about two seconds. Everything else about a gender
reveal, every conversation and every arrangement, exists to make those two
seconds happen in front of the right people with a camera already running.

We have photographed two of them on this roof. They are the reason this page
can describe the thing rather than imagine it.

### Someone has to know, and it is not going to be you

The whole service is built around one person: whoever is holding the result.
Usually a family member, sometimes the clinic.

The planning conversation happens with them, on a channel that does not reach
you. They agree the result, the colour and the timing with us. Neither of us
tells you, and nothing we send you before the day gives it away.

If nobody can hold it, say so when you enquire. There is more than one way to
arrange this, and we would rather suggest one than let the plan quietly depend
on a person who turns out not to be available.

### The two we have photographed looked nothing like each other

Same roof. Same balloon garland in pink, blue and white. Same lit letters
spelling BABY, the same teddy bears, the same white flowers, the same wooden
steps and the same strip of artificial grass. Two completely different sets of
photographs, because of the hour.

The first was shot in the middle of the day. Hard blue sky, sharp shadows, the
three pyramids crisp and pale behind the town roofs of Giza, the balloon
colours reading bright and saturated. It is a cheerful, high-contrast, very
awake-looking set of pictures.

The second was shot at dusk. The sky went pink and gold, the haze softened the
pyramids until they read as shapes rather than stone, and the bulbs in the
BABY letters came on and became part of the photograph instead of a prop
sitting in daylight. Warmer, quieter, and the only one of the two where the
lighting in the decoration does any work.

Neither is better. They are different photographs of the same setup, and the
difference is entirely the hour you book. It is worth deciding which one you
are imagining before you pick a time, because it is the single choice that
changes the pictures most.

### What actually happened at those two reveals

At the daytime one, the result came out of a plain white box standing on the
deck, with a printed sign on the front. The lid came off and helium balloons
went up out of it. Pink. The photographs either side of that frame are the two
of them watching the balloons leave, which is the part people forget to
picture when they are planning the box.

At the dusk one, the answer arrived as a script foil balloon reading *girl*,
held up in front of the lit letters with the pyramids behind.

Read those as what was agreed for those two dates, not as a menu. They are
the only honest way to show you what this looks like, and they are not an
offer, for the reason in the next section.

### Why we will not promise you a prop

It is the venue's building, and anything that pops, sprays, burns or drifts is
their call. It can differ by date, by wind, and by who else is on the roof
that evening.

So we ask them before we agree it with you, never after. Tell us what you are
imagining when you enquire and we will come back with what the roof will
permit on your date. Publishing a list we have not confirmed is how somebody
arrives expecting an effect that was never possible, and that is a worse
afternoon than being told no in advance.

The same applies to a cake or props you want to bring yourself, and to flowers
and additional styling, which come through suppliers we work with rather than
sitting in stock. Each one is confirmed separately once the date is held.

### Everyone who came is in the photographs

A reveal with nobody else in the frame is a different event, and a smaller one.

What gets covered is the moment and the faces of the people watching it, the
approach while nobody has said anything yet, the family around the reveal
including whoever travelled for it, and directed portraits before or after
while everybody is still in one place and still dressed for it.

How many people can come is a venue question rather than ours. Tell us the
number when you enquire and we will confirm what the rooftop can take on your
date.

### Two weeks, not two days

Allow a fortnight or more. It is not a formality. The decoration, the balloons
and anything coming through a supplier have to be arranged properly rather
than found on the morning.

The event itself usually runs ninety minutes to two hours, depending on how
many people come and whether portraits are part of it.

### What it costs

There is no published price for a gender reveal, and that is deliberate rather
than evasive. What it costs depends on how many people come, which decoration
you choose and whether you want it filmed, so you get a figure in writing
before you commit to anything.

The quotation comes back after we have confirmed the roof, not before. How and
when you pay is confirmed in writing with it. No page on this website takes a
payment or asks for card details, and an enquiry does not reserve the roof.

### If you want it filmed

A reveal is one of the few things we photograph that is arguably better as
footage, because the sound is half of it. A cinematic film of the reveal, slow
motion of the moment itself, and vertical cuts for the people who are going to
watch this on a phone are all shot and edited in house rather than through a
supplier, so they are arranged with the rest of the session and quoted with it.

### The thing most people wish they had asked about

A flying dress session for the pregnancy, on the same roof and the same
evening. Everyone is already there and already dressed, and it is something we
do ourselves rather than arrange through anybody else.

## FAQs

**Who finds out first?**
Nobody in the couple. The result goes from whoever is holding it to us, and
the planning happens with them on a channel that does not reach you.

**Can we do a confetti cannon, smoke or a balloon pop?**
Tell us what you are imagining and we will ask the venue for your date. We do
not publish a list of permitted effects, because what the roof allows can
change with the date, the wind and who else is up there that evening.

**Is it better in the day or at sunset?**
They produce genuinely different photographs. Daylight gives you hard blue sky
and saturated colour. Dusk gives you a pink and gold sky, softer pyramids, and
lit letters that actually read as lit. Pick the one you are already imagining.

**How many people can come?**
That is the venue's answer rather than ours. Give us the number when you
enquire and we will confirm it for your date.

**How far ahead do we need to book?**
Two weeks or more, mainly so the decoration and any props can be arranged
properly.

**How much is it?**
There is no published price. It depends on numbers, decoration and whether you
want video, and you get a written quotation once the roof is confirmed.
