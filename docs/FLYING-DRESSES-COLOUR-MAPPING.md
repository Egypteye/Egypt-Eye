## What the visual inspection found

**Purple is Lavender, not Orchid.** The Purple folder's frames are a vivid
violet-purple, shot on the rooftop set. `dress-lavender.jpg` is the same
violet-purple on what appears to be the same set. Orchid, by contrast, is a
magenta-fuchsia — a different hue family altogether.

**Magenta is Orchid.** Deep fuchsia-magenta in both. Once Purple is settled
as Lavender, Orchid is the only magenta left in the collection, and the
frames agree.

**Em. Green is Emerald; Green is Verdant.** "Em." is almost certainly short
for Emerald, and the frames bear it out: Em. Green is a bright jewel green
with a satin sheen matching `dress-emerald.jpg`, while Green is a deeper
bottle-forest green matching `dress-verdant.jpg`. Had these been swapped the
error would have been invisible in text and obvious in a photograph.

**Pink is Hot Pink, and the evidence is unusually strong.** The frame pulled
from the Pink folder and the site's published `dress-hot-pink.jpg` appear to
be *the same photograph*. That is not a colour resemblance, it is a match,
and it tells us the site's dress images were sourced from these folders in
the first place. It also raises the confidence of every other row.

So Yellow is one of three things, and only you know which:

1. **The same dress as Ginger**, photographed in an earlier session and

2. **A second, slightly different marigold** that is still in the wardrobe

It matters because the one article where this cannot be fudged is the article
about choosing a colour. If Yellow is Ginger, its four frames are extra

using it to illustrate Ginger would show a customer a dress they cannot book.

**Until you say, Yellow is excluded from every article.** That is the safe
default and it costs four photographs.

## What has not been done

**Provenance has not yet been checked on these files.** Viewing a frame to
judge its colour is not the same as clearing it for publication. Every image
actually placed in an article gets the byte-level check first, classified on
the evidence rather than on the presence of any one vendor's name:

| Class | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Original photography | EXIF present, no C2PA generative assertion, no generator |
| Edited photography | `c2pa.edited`, or `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`, or a named editor such as Photoshop or Lightroom |
| Generated imagery | `c2pa.created` with `digitalSourceType: trainedAlgorithmicMedia` |
| Uncertain | anything that does not fall cleanly into the above |

Per your instruction of 20 November: an OpenAI manifest on its own does not
make an image generated. `c2pa.created` plus `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` does.
An OpenAI manifest attached to an edit of a real photograph is edited
photography and is labelled as such. Only the generated class is barred from
anything presented as a real customer experience.
