# Egyptian oils — research, and what is still unknown

Status: **research complete, catalogue NOT publishable.** Written 15 October 2026.

This is the evidence behind the Essence Oils collection. Every row says where it
came from and how far it can be trusted. Nothing here may be put on the site as
fact unless this file says it is one.

---

## 1. The headline finding

**Egyptian Chambers is not our supplier.** The owner confirmed we buy in Cairo
and that the Egyptian Chambers site is a reference for *what this kind of
product is*, not for what we sell.

That matters more than anything else in this document:

- Their product names may not be our product names.
- Their prices are in **pounds sterling** (£21–£114), which is a UK retail
  price for a UK customer. It is not our cost and it is not our price.
- Their scent notes describe **their** blends. A bottle labelled "Jasmine" in
  a Cairo shop is not guaranteed to be the same liquid.

So their catalogue is used here for one purpose only: to establish what this
category of product generally is, and to show what a complete product record
looks like. **Not one of their figures has been copied into our CMS.**

## 2. These are fragrance oils, not essential oils

This is the single most useful thing the research settled, and it is the trap
the brief warned about.

Egyptian Chambers describes its range as *"authentic Egyptian fragrance oils"*
— fragrance oils, their own words. Their product pages publish a **note
pyramid** (top/heart/base) rather than a botanical origin, which is how a
perfumer's blend is described, not how a distilled essential oil is.

Their "Sandalwood" is described as yielded from the bark of *Santalum album*
and their "Frankincense" as a *Boswellia* blend — but neither page states a
distillation method, a concentration, a carrier oil, or any third-party test.

**Therefore: a bottle labelled Lemongrass, Peppermint, Sandalwood, Rose or
Frankincense must NOT be described on our site as a pure essential oil.** We
have no evidence of that for their stock and none at all for ours.

Two claims on their site — *"free from alcohol and chemicals"* and *"lasts up
to 12 hours"* — are the company's own marketing assertions. They are not
independently verified and must not be repeated as fact on our pages.

### What this means for our own stock

Unknown. We have not seen an ingredient list, a safety data sheet, or an
allergen declaration from the Cairo supplier. Until we do:

- Product type stays **unverified** on every record.
- The product-type filter is **not shown**, because a filter built on unknown
  data is worse than no filter.
- No page claims a product is natural, organic, pure, therapeutic, or
  hypoallergenic, and none claims a health benefit.

### Two name corrections the research turned up

- The supplier sells **"Nefertari"** — a separate product from the
  **"Nefertiti"** on the photographed bottle. Both names appear. Whether these
  are two products or one renamed is **unconfirmed**.
- **No "Cypress" product was found** anywhere on the supplier's site.
  "Papyrus" does exist, in their Blends category.

### A limit on this research

This sandbox cannot resolve `egyptianchambersoils.com` — the environment's
network policy blocks it, and `getaddrinfo` fails. Every fact above was
gathered through server-side web search, which returns summaries of those
pages rather than the pages themselves. Several of the indexed copies are
**1.5 to 1.7 years old**, so prices and stock on them may be stale.

No page of that site was read directly. Nothing here should be treated as a
live quote from the supplier, and the figures are included only to show that
their pricing is in the wrong currency and the wrong market to reuse.

## 6. What is needed before any of this can be published

From the Cairo supplier, per product:

1. The exact product name as they label it.
2. Whether it is an essential oil, a fragrance oil, a perfume oil or a blend.
3. The scent notes they stand behind.
4. The bottle sizes they actually sell.
5. Ingredients and allergens, ideally a safety data sheet.
6. Our cost, so a retail price can be set.

From Egypt Eye:

7. Our retail price per product and size.
8. Photographs we own, or written permission for the supplier's.
9. The shop's address and opening hours, for the private-visit flow.
10. Whether the unreadable "…rus" bottle is Papyrus, Cyprus or Cypress.
11. Whether Nefertiti and Nefertari are both stocked.
